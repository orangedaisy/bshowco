## Inspiration

I've been a fan of the Chicago Cubs for ages, going all the way back to
Slammin' Sammy Sosa and the home run record chase of '98. I especially love
listening to the fantastic Cubs Radio crew of Pat Hughes and Ron Coomer. This
year, my wife and I decided to start scoring games at home to help keep us off
our phones and engage with the action on the diamond. But which scorecard to
choose? Between [Chalk and Ink](https://www.chalkandinkbaseball.com/shop/),
[Numbers Game](https://www.numbersgame.co/collections/scorebooks), and [7-2
Double Play](https://www.72doubleplay.com/) (among many others), I couldn't
make up my mind. So I made my own.

My scorecard is focused primarily on the players and plays. I don't
particularly care to keep track of individual stats, league standings, pitch
counts, umpire rosters, etc. so I left those out. I _am_ interested in how a
particular play transpired, so I borrowed the [layout from Chalk and
Ink](https://www.chalkandinkbaseball.com/how-to-score/) for some scaffolding to
draw base paths, record outs, and track RBIs.

I also really like the [Small
Ball](https://www.72doubleplay.com/small-ball-scorebook/) idea from 7-2 Double
Play, so I got rid of distinct inning columns and let the game flow from one
box to the next. A thicker scribble between adjacent boxes is all it takes to
indicate the next inning. A fun side-effect of this approach is the scorecard
can begin to resemble a race&mdash;one team running away with more plate
appearances becomes obvious, with added drama if they're unable to convert
those extra chances to runs.

My final design decision was to build this scorecard for use on my iPad. I like
the idea of a physical book for scorekeeping, but I knew I'd have a hard time
living within the confines of a piece of paper. Sometimes plays need to be
amended, sometimes I get the substitution wrong, and sometimes I just have to
rewrite something a dozen times because my handwriting isn't the best. With
Goodnotes, I can pinch-to-zoom and rewrite to my heart's content without
littering the page with eraser marks.

A simple color palette based on the Cubs team colors and the wonderful [Grand
Sans](https://simplebits.shop/products/grandsans/) and
[Scorekard](https://simplebits.shop/products/scorekard/) typefaces from [Dan
Cederholm](https://simplebits.com/) tie everything together.

## In action

### Cubs at Mets, June 25, 2026

This game revealed one flaw with the Small Ball format: where to put the
Automatic Runner in extra innings? You can't really work backwards when
everything has been running unbroken to that point. I stuck the AR in the last
column, but I'm going to play around with other ideas as this keeps coming up.
These cards also don't have the final fonts and icons as I was still
stress-testing the layout and other design elements before I took that final
step.

### Padres at Cubs, July 1, 2026

This was an incredible game as a Cubs fan. I actually lost track of the Cubs'
substitutions in the later innings so I wasn't able to record exactly when each
player entered the game. The scorecard and my process was pretty settled by
this point. I use the palette's dark blue to track bascially everything. Red
marks important notes and substitutions. Green ink goes above the basepaths to
mark whose plate appearance is responsible for the runner's movement, as well
as any RBIs that batter recorded.

## Downloads

### Digital

These scorecards are the same dimensions as the "Goodnotes Standard" paper at
455.04 &times; 588.41pt, according to [Measurement
Stuff](https://measurementstuff.com/goodnotes-standard-paper-size/) (why
Goodnotes can't just share this info I'll never know). My wife and I use these
on our 11&Prime; and 12.9&Prime; iPads, but your mileage may vary depending on
your specific device and notation app.

[Showalter Scorecard &ndash; Digital
(PDF)](/assets/files/keeping_score/showalter-scorecard-digital.pdf)

### Print

Same setup as the digital version, but with a CMYK color profile and no
background color. I found these print at a nice, comfortable size when allowed
to fill a 8.5&Prime; &times; 11&Prime; sheet of printer paper in landscape.
